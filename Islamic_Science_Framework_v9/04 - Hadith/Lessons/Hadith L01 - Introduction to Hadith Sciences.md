## 🗺 Tasawwur

> This lesson introduces the science of Ḥadīth: its definition, the structure of a ḥadīth (isnād + matn), and the basic grading categories (ṣaḥīḥ, ḥasan, ḍaʿīf, mawḍūʿ) that determine whether a report is accepted or rejected.

---

## 🔴 Masāʾil — The Spine (EXACTLY 5)

| Dot | Type | Masʾalah |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 | **Foundation** | The Sunnah is the second source of legislation after the Qurʾān, and ḥadīth science is the methodology that preserves its authenticity |
| 🟡 | **Proof** | "Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it; and whatever he forbids you, abstain from it" (al-Ḥashr 59:7) — obedience to the Prophet ﷺ is obligatory |
| 🟢 | **Application** | Every ḥadīth has an isnād (chain of narrators) and a matn (text) — a ḥadīth is graded ṣaḥīḥ only when it meets all five conditions of acceptance |
| 🟠 | **Protection** | Accepting weak or fabricated ḥadīths leads to innovations in worship and incorrect rulings — the grading system exists to prevent this |
| ⚫ | **Bridge** | Ḥadīth science serves all other sciences: ʿAqīdah relies on authentic reports for creedal matters, Fiqh derives rulings from them, and Tafsīr uses them for asbāb al-nuzūl |

---

## 🟡 Speed Spine

> **This lesson establishes that:**
> Ḥadīth science protects the Sunnah through a rigorous grading methodology based on isnād and matn analysis.

> **The evidence is:**
> al-Ḥashr 59:7 establishes the authority of the Sunnah, and the scholars developed the isnād system to preserve it.

> **This matters because:**
> Without ḥadīth verification, anyone could attribute anything to the Prophet ﷺ — the grading system is what keeps the religion protected from fabrication.

## 🔴 Taṭbīq

### 🎯 Scenario 1

> Someone shares a "ḥadīth" on social media with no source. The correct response is to verify: Who narrated it? Through what chain? Is it in the authentic collections? A fabricated ḥadīth attributed to the Prophet ﷺ is a grave sin — "Whoever lies upon me intentionally, let him take his seat in the Fire."

### 🎯 Scenario 2

> Case A: A ḥadīth in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī with a continuous chain of reliable narrators (ṣaḥīḥ). Case B: A report found only in a weak collection with a broken chain (ḍaʿīf). Both are called "ḥadīth" but only the first can be used as evidence for rulings and ʿaqīdah.

### 🎯 Scenario 3

> Imagine you heard a rumor that the CEO said something important. Would you act on it from a random stranger, or would you trace it back through trusted people who actually heard the CEO speak? The isnād system is exactly that — tracing every word back through reliable witnesses to the Prophet ﷺ himself.

### 🎯 Scenario 4

> Before sharing any ḥadīth this week — in conversation, messages, or social media — I will verify its grading first using a reliable app or book.
