## 🗺 Tasawwur

> This lesson introduces Fiqh as the science of practical rulings and establishes the five-category classification system (wājib, mustaḥabb, mubāḥ, makrūh, ḥarām) that governs every human action.

---

## 🔴 Masāʾil — The Spine (EXACTLY 5)

| Dot | Type | Masʾalah |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 | **Foundation** | Fiqh is the detailed understanding of Sharīʿah rulings derived from their evidences — it governs every action a Muslim performs |
| 🟡 | **Proof** | "It is not for the believers to go forth all at once. Of every group, a party should remain to gain understanding (tafaqquh) in the religion" (al-Tawbah 9:122) |
| 🟢 | **Application** | Every human action falls into one of five categories: wājib (obligatory), mustaḥabb (recommended), mubāḥ (permissible), makrūh (disliked), ḥarām (prohibited) |
| 🟠 | **Protection** | Treating a wājib as sunnah leads to neglect; treating a sunnah as wājib leads to extremism — precision in classification prevents both |
| ⚫ | **Bridge** | Fiqh rulings are built on ʿAqīdah foundations — the obligation to pray comes from Tawḥīd (worshipping Allah alone) |

---

## 🟡 Speed Spine

> **This lesson establishes that:**
> Fiqh classifies every human action into five categories, and precision in this classification is essential to correct worship.

> **The evidence is:**
> al-Tawbah 9:122 commands a group to remain behind specifically to gain deep understanding (tafaqquh) in the religion.

> **This matters because:**
> Without knowing the ruling of an action, you cannot worship Allah correctly — you risk neglecting the obligatory or innovating in the prohibited.

## 🔴 Taṭbīq — Application

### 🎯 Scenario 1 — Error Detection

> Someone says: "I don't need to study Fiqh — I just follow my heart." This is refuted by al-Tawbah 9:122 which commands seeking understanding. The five rulings exist precisely because human intuition alone cannot determine what Allah requires — revelation and scholarly derivation are needed.

### 🎯 Scenario 2 — Distinction

> Case A: Praying Fajr — this is wājib; abandoning it is sinful. Case B: Praying Ḍuḥā — this is mustaḥabb; abandoning it carries no sin. Both are prayer, but confusing their categories leads to either negligence or undue burden.

### 🎯 Scenario 3 — Teaching Test

> Imagine a traffic system with only "go" and "stop" lights. It would be chaos. Real traffic has five signals: must go, should go, neutral, should stop, must stop. Islamic law works the same way — every action has one of five rulings so you know exactly where you stand.

### 🎯 Scenario 4 — Personal Application

> I will review my daily worship this week and identify one action I've been treating as optional that is actually obligatory, and one action I've been stressing about that is actually just recommended.
