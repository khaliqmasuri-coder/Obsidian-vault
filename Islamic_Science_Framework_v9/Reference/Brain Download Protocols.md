# 🧠 Brain-Download Protocols — Techniques for Deep Retention

---

## ① The Feynman Method — Required for Every Lesson at ③ Fahm

> [!danger] Complete all 4 steps. Skipping any step means you are memorizing, not understanding.

### Step 1 — Take Your Notes Away

Close the framework. Write the lesson concept on a blank page in plain English as if explaining to someone who has never heard of it.

### Step 2 — Find the Gaps

Where did you get stuck? Where did you use a term without being able to explain it? Those are your gaps.

### Step 3 — Go Back to the Source

Return to your notes **only** for the gap areas. Study ONLY those. Then close again.

### Step 4 — Simplify Further

Replace every technical term with a plain-language equivalent. If you cannot, you do not yet understand it — you are only labeling it.

> [!caution] You are done when you can explain the full concept **without using a single technical term you cannot also define simply.**

---

## ② The Teach-Back Protocol

> [!danger] After every lesson, you must do ONE of the following **within 48 hours** (not optional):

### 🗣️ Option A — Speak It

Record a 5-minute voice memo explaining the lesson to an imaginary student. Play it back. Did you go blank anywhere? That gap needs work.

### 👤 Option B — Teach a Person

Explain the lesson to someone (Muslim or non-Muslim, it doesn't matter). Their confusion reveals your unclear spots.

### ✍️ Option C — Write It Cold

Without notes, write a 1-page summary of the lesson. Then compare to your notes. Mark every gap in red.

> [!caution] If you cannot complete any option — the lesson is NOT done. Go back. You have information. You do not yet have **knowledge**.

---

## ③ The Concept Map — Draw Your Understanding

For every major topic (not every lesson), draw a concept map on a blank page:

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| **CENTER** | The core principle of the topic (e.g., "Tawḥīd al-Ulūhiyyah") |
| **BRANCHES** | The 5 masāʾil radiating outward with their color codes |
| **LEAVES** | The dalīl and application for each masʾalah |
| **ROOTS** | The uṣūl principles that ground the topic |
| **CONNECTIONS** | Lines between branches showing cross-subject links |

> [!tip] The act of drawing forces your brain to organize relationships — this is where fragmented notes become structured knowledge.
