## 🗺 Tasawwur

> This lesson introduces Ādāb as the science of character refinement and establishes Ikhlāṣ (sincerity) as the foundational station upon which all other stations of the heart are built.

---

## 🔴 Masāʾil — The Spine (EXACTLY 5)

| Dot | Type | Masʾalah |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 | **Foundation** | Ādāb is the science that transforms knowledge from information in the mind to character in the heart — it is what makes ʿilm beneficial |
| 🟡 | **Proof** | "And they were not commanded except to worship Allah, sincere to Him in religion" (al-Bayyinah 98:5) — Ikhlāṣ is a condition, not a bonus |
| 🟢 | **Application** | Ikhlāṣ means purifying every intention for Allah alone — checking before every act of worship and study: who am I doing this for? |
| 🟠 | **Protection** | Knowledge without Ikhlāṣ becomes a proof against you, not for you — the first three judged on the Day of Judgment include a scholar who studied for reputation |
| ⚫ | **Bridge** | Ikhlāṣ connects directly to Tawḥīd al-Ulūhiyyah (singling out Allah in worship) and is the internal counterpart to external acts of Fiqh |

---

## 🟡 Speed Spine

> **This lesson establishes that:**
> Ādāb is the transformation of knowledge into character, and Ikhlāṣ is the non-negotiable foundation of every act of worship and study.

> **The evidence is:**
> al-Bayyinah 98:5 commands worship with sincerity — Ikhlāṣ is a condition of acceptance, not an optional virtue.

> **This matters because:**
> Without Ikhlāṣ, even correct knowledge and correct practice are rejected — the intention determines whether an act counts for you or against you.

## 🔴 Taṭbīq

### 🎯 Scenario 1

> A student studies Islamic knowledge enthusiastically but finds himself excited mainly when others praise his learning. The ḥadīth of the first three judged (Muslim) warns that a scholar who studied for reputation is among the first dragged to the Fire. The correct approach: constant self-audit of intention.

### 🎯 Scenario 2

> Case A: A person teaches a class to benefit the students, sincerely for Allah. Case B: A person teaches the same class, same content, but their primary motivation is to be seen as knowledgeable. Externally identical — internally, one is accepted and the other is rejected.

### 🎯 Scenario 3

> Imagine you bake a beautiful cake for someone you love. Then imagine you bake the exact same cake, but only because you want to post it on social media. The cake looks the same, but the first one has love baked in and the second is hollow. Ikhlāṣ is the love in the cake — without it, the action looks right but is empty.

### 🎯 Scenario 4

> Before every study session this week, I will pause for 30 seconds and ask myself: "Am I studying this for Allah, or for how it makes me look?" If I catch the wrong intention, I will renew it before proceeding.
